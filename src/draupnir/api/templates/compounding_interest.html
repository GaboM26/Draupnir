Compounding and discounting

Both valuation models rest on one idea: money grows at a rate, and money expected later is worth less today.

Growing a value

A value V growing at rate r per year for n years becomes V × (1 + r)n. At 9% a year, 100 becomes about 154 in five years and 237 in ten.

Discounting a future value

Run backwards, the same formula gives a present value: Vtoday = Vfuture ÷ (1 + r)n. The rate r is the return you require. A higher required return makes future cash worth less now.

How the models use it

  • DCF grows free cash flow for several years, discounts each year back to today, and adds a terminal value for every year after: CF × (1 + g) ÷ (r − g), which needs r greater than g.
  • P/E grows earnings per share, multiplies by a typical P/E ratio to get a future price, and discounts that price back to today.
